What changed from the uniform prototype

The lattice version was a perfect 7-ary tree over an empty field, so every cell was the same size and every fold looked identical. This one starts from a generated vault and derives the hierarchy from its links, so cluster sizes are log-normal, some cells hold seven children and some hold two, and the ladder is deeper over a dense island than over a sparse one. The raggedness is the data, not noise added on top.

Coarsening never crosses a component. That single rule is what makes the overview read as a handful of islands instead of one blob, and it guarantees a dashed cluster can never mix two unrelated topics — the disorientation complaint, fixed by construction rather than by tuning.

Orphans are the exception. Each is its own component, so left alone they would stay thousands of separate dots forever and eat the whole budget. They get bundled into one orphan pile that coarsens spatially — which is also how they read to a person: not a topic, just the unfiled drawer. 

generate(seed, spec)      // vault_synth.zig
    ↓  notes + links
components()              // islands, + orphan pile
    ↓
coarsen(arity)            // ≤7 linked children
    ↓  ragged ladder
place()                   // hex slots, ℓ·19.1066°
    ↓
select(zoom, budget)      // biggest-first, capped
    ↓
lift(links) → draw

The dashed rings are the coarsening ladder's interior nodes, drawn directly. There is no second structure and no bake — which is the property every rejected handoff in galaxy-lod.md lacked.

Four rules I left out, and what each one broke

Getting the dive to actually reach notes took four fixes. Three of them are rules already written down in organic-lod.md that I had simply not implemented — and each one failed in exactly the way that document says it does, which is a good sign the rules are load bearing rather than historical.

Missing ruleSymptom
Cull inside select, not at draw The whole vault sat on the open-candidate list, so the budget test never passed. The dive stalled about a fifth of the way in and never showed a note. Children are placed inside their parent's radius, so the off-screen test is exact and cheap.
Level-uniform opening Opening biggest-first until the budget ran out left identical neighbours resolved differently by visit order — the half-fine/half-coarse seam. Open a whole level or none of it.
Never force-close an open cell at the wall Closing freed budget, which allowed reopening, which exceeded it again. Max zoom juddered. Refusing only new opens fixed it: parked at the wall, the ring count is now identical for 19 consecutive frames.
A note is a fixed screen size Scaling notes with zoom like masses turned the close view into overlapping discs with the links buried underneath. Only masses are count-and-zoom scaled, and soft-capped so a cell the budget refused can't inflate to fill the screen.

The fourth is not in any document and is worth writing down: the far end of the zoom range has to overshoot the point where the budget could just about hold the visible notes. Landing exactly there stalls one level short, because opening that last level costs seven times what the level above did. Sizing max zoom off the budget, then multiplying by ~1.7, is what makes the dive end on notes instead of rings.

One thing the prototype changed my mind about

A globally rigid hex lattice and real cluster sizes fight each other. If every cell must land on a shared lattice point, a 3-note cluster and a 300-note cluster claim the same slot pitch, and you are back to the uniform field — the lattice wins and the data disappears.

What works here instead: hex slots are local to the parent and scaled to its radius, with the fold's 19.1066° rotation kept. You get the hex language — centre plus six, nesting, the turn on each dive — while cell spacing follows √count. Worth deciding deliberately, because it is a real departure from "every node lands exactly on a background grid dot" in hex.zig.

The knobs, and one that surprised me

vault_synth.zig had notes, shape, average degree, orphan fraction and island size bounds. It now also has the two below — bridge_frac (default 0.0, so existing benches are unchanged) and a folder tree derived from the generated components with a folder_leak_frac of misfiled notes, replacing the random fakePaths that was feeding noise to layout_full.zig's folder-cohesion force.

KnobWhat it modelsWhy it matters
bridges links between two different islands, biased toward high-degree endpoints Real vaults are not cleanly separable — and the graph is far more fragile to this than it looks. See the sweep below.
size spread island_max, driving the log-uniform size draw Already a field in Spec, but not exposed as a bench axis. It is the single strongest control over how ragged the ladder gets.

Sweeping that knob at 2,193 notes / seed 7 / islands produced the thing I did not expect, and it is the reason a generator like this is worth having at all:

Four extra links per thousand notes take the largest component from 18% of the vault to 52%. That is a percolation threshold sitting right where a real vault lives — a handful of index notes that link across topics is enough to fuse most of a vault into one component. Anything that treats "islands" as a structural given, including the never-coarsen-across-components rule this prototype relies on, is resting on a knife edge: the same vault is 29 tidy islands or one 52% blob depending on a few links. Worth measuring on real vaults before the LOD leans on it.

The folder change matters for a similar reason. fakePaths assigned paths at random, so layout_full.zig's folder-cohesion force (folder_k = 0.42, scoring same-directory 1.0 and parent/child 0.55) has been measured against noise on every synth bench it has ever run. The scale plan wants folder path promoted from a force to a coarsening prior — that cannot be evaluated at all until the generator emits folders that agree with the links it generated.
